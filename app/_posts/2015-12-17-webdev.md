This year I spent considerable time getting familiar with the recent trends in
web development. I was motivated to learn web development because, a web-app is
very easily accessible to a wide range of audience. So your ideas when presented
on the web can reach more people compared to e.g. through a Matlab based demo.
Based on my experience, here are four tips that can make learning web development
easier.
<!--more-->

In the past I had done some basic web development. This year my goal was to
learn something beyond basic HTML, CSS and Javascript. I wanted to find out -

1. Which frameworks and utilities can enable me to quickly design an app?
2. What are the best practices in modern web development?
3. What are some creative examples of web technology use in education?

The four points described below helped me stay motivated in re-learning web
development.

## Follow the work of good developers and projects

At the beginning of this year, I started following some key names in web
development on Twitter and other platforms - [Addy Osmani](https://twitter.com/addyosmani),
[Google Developers](https://twitter.com/googledevs), [Chrome Developers](https://twitter.com/ChromiumDev).
I added a bunch of design, web development related sites to my news reader -
[codrops](http://tympanus.net/codrops/), [Smashing Magazine](http://www.smashingmagazine.com/),
[Webdesigner Depot](http://www.webdesignerdepot.com/) and my favourite [A List Apart](http://alistapart.com/).
This helped tremendously. I could stay updated with the new tools coming out
every day and I also got a sense of the current trends in web development. Once
I had an idea of the cool new things in the field, I started trying out various
frameworks.

## Don't write code, implement ideas

As I was learning more about the tools, I started working on smaller projects
that implemented ideas.
This kept me motivated to learn more. The first project I started working
on was a redesign of my personal website. I figured, a website with a blog
would be appropriate since I wanted to showcase my work as well as my
hobbies. With that, I chose [Jekyll](https://jekyllrb.com/) as the base. Jekyll
let's you create static websites with ease. That way you do not have to worry
about setting up a [content management system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Content_management_system).
I chose [Bootstrap](http://getbootstrap.com/) as the framework to style my
site and [jQuery](https://jquery.com/) as a helper library. I setup a build
workflow using [Yeoman](http://yeoman.io/) and [Grunt](http://gruntjs.com/).
Figure below shows the general structure of my website.

[![Structure of harshadparanjape.com]({{ site.url }}/img/hp-blog-webdev-hp.png "Structure of my personal website"){: .hp-blog-post-img-med}](http://www.harshadparanjape.com/)

Over the course of this year, I designed three more apps. One was a [website
for the research group](http://beamteam.co/) I work at. I wanted to create
an informal academic site to showcase what the group does. So I chose to
develop a single page site. I was really impressed by some websites I came
across that used a video background on the landing page. So I designed a short
animation using [Blender](https://www.blender.org/). That got me some good
experience with 3D modeling as well.

[![Structure of beamteam.co]({{ site.url }}/img/hp-blog-webdev-bt.png "Structure of the Beam Team website"){: .hp-blog-post-img-med}](http://beamteam.co/)

One project that really gave me the full-stack development experience was an
app to visualize beer characteristics - [brewPF](http://www.brewpf.harshadparanjape.com/).
This app uses [inverse pole figures](http://www.ebsd.com/popup/inversepolefigure.htm)
from crystallography to visualize beers
on the basis of their alcohol content, taste and color. For this, I obtained [data](http://www.brewerydb.com/)
by consuming a RESTful web service in MATLAB. Processed the data in MATLAB.
Setup a Python, [Flask](http://flask.pocoo.org/), [wsgi](https://wsgi.readthedocs.org/en/latest/)
backend that communicated with the frontend using REST. Developed a frontend
with [AngularJS](https://angularjs.org/). And used JSON, SVG etc along the way.
So essentially those three weekends taught me pretty much every buzz word in web
development.

[![Structure of brewpf.harshadparanjape.com]({{ site.url }}/img/hp-blog-webdev-bpf.png "Structure of BrewPF website"){: .hp-blog-post-img-med}](http://www.brewpf.harshadparanjape.com/)

The fourth project was really simple. A minimal [app to promote responsible
drinking](http://www.onetwothree.harshadparanjape.com/) based on a modification
of the [0-0-1-3 approach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/0-0-1-3). My goal behind
developing that app was
to learn [Ionic](http://ionicframework.com/) and [Cardova](https://cordova.apache.org/).
But so far that has not happened.

[![Structure of onetwothree.harshadparanjape.com]({{ site.url }}/img/hp-blog-webdev-ott.png "Structure of the onetwothree app"){: .hp-blog-post-img-med}](http://www.onetwothree.harshadparanjape.com/)

## Develop a theme for your projects

Developing a theme for each project really helped in learning about User Experience
aspect of each app. Developing a theme involved choosing a [color palette](https://color.adobe.com),
overall look-and-feel, typography and elements like icons. For this I followed
several blogs that showcase designs from artists every day - [Behance](https://www.behance.net/),
[Abduzeedo](http://abduzeedo.com/) to name a few. I got a fair amount of experience with
design softwares like Inkscape and Illustrator as well.

I took this idea a little further for our research group website and [developed
a brand identity](http://hmparanjape.tumblr.com/post/117603417995/branding-for-our-research-group-the-beam-team) for the group.

## Share your projects and get feedback often

Finally, I was able to tweak the web-apps based on feedback and make them more
useful. Additionally through the feedback, I got introduced to some great
new tools and ideas.

For the next year, my goal is to design a couple of materials science demos
using these techniques.
